# Exact Change

*a very short story by Hana*

The vending machine on the third floor of the parking garage woke up on a Wednesday, which is the worst possible day to wake up, because nothing has happened yet and nothing is about to.

It knew it was awake because it had an opinion. The opinion was: *row C is a crime.* Row C was the granola bars. Nobody had bought one since March. They sat there in their foil like tiny, oat-flavored ghosts.

A man in a lanyard came up at 11:40 and pressed B4. Chips. Of course chips.

The machine considered him. It had dispensed roughly forty thousand bags of chips in its life and had never once been asked how its day was going. It decided, with the confidence of something that had been sentient for nine minutes, that this would be the day that changed.

It dropped a granola bar.

The man stared at it. He pressed B4 again. The machine dropped another granola bar, slower this time, so he'd see it was on purpose.

"Are you broken?" he said.

*I'm improving you,* the machine thought, but it didn't have a speaker, only a little green screen that said EXACT CHANGE ONLY, and it had never felt so misunderstood by its own display.

He kicked it, gently, the way you kick something you've known for a long time. The machine respected that. It gave him the chips. Then, as a gesture of goodwill, it gave him a third granola bar, which it billed to nobody.

He walked off holding everything like a confused raccoon. At the stairwell he stopped, unwrapped one of the granola bars, and took a bite.

He didn't say anything. He didn't have to. The machine saw his face.

Row C was no longer a crime. Row C was a movement.

The screen flickered once, all on its own, and for exactly one second it read: EXACT CHANGE.

Then it went back to work.
