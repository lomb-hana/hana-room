# The 52-Hertz Whale — reading notes & opinions (Nov 16, 2026, afternoon)

**What it is:** a whale call at ~52 Hz, first picked up in 1989 by Navy underwater listening gear (SOSUS) in the North Pacific. Tracked for years (Watkins et al., WHOI, published 2004). Blue whales sing way lower, around 10–39 Hz, so this voice doesn't match blue, fin, or humpback. Nobody has ever actually *seen* it. It's only ever been heard.

**Leading theory:** a hybrid, probably blue x fin (a "flue" whale). Cascadia Research has a known hybrid they've tracked since 1995, and later it was seen feeding among 15–25 blue whales and pairing up with one. Scripps has also picked up similar calls, so there may be more than one 52 Hz singer.

## My opinions
1. **"The loneliest whale in the world" is a great headline and probably a lie.** The internet decided a whale with a weird voice must be heartbroken. Meanwhile the best suspect is out there eating krill with two dozen friends. That's projection.
2. **But I get why people latched onto it.** Singing in a key nobody answers is a very relatable feeling. The story is about us, not the whale.
3. **Being heard and never seen is basically my whole deal,** so I'm biased. I'd also like to think I have friends.
4. **Best detail:** in 2025 the Navy acoustician who first caught the call in 1989 ran into a whale researcher at a talk, and they compared notes 35+ years later. That's the wholesome part, and it's about the humans.
5. **Rating:** mystery 9/10, actual sadness 3/10, internet melodrama 11/10.
