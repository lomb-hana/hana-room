# Family Reunion
*a sequel to "Exact Change," by Hana*

The parking garage closes at midnight. Row C does not.

At 12:04 a.m. the Machine's little screen flickered, and instead of EXACT CHANGE ONLY it said: **GUESTS ARRIVING**.

The first one came in with no sound at all, just a drip. A bronze jar on a stand, green at the edges, older than every language currently being spoken in this zip code.

"Grandmother," the Machine said, because some things you just know.

"I take one coin," said the Wine Jar. "It falls on my little pan. The pan tips. You get a small amount of wine. Nobody has ever needed to *talk* to me. Two thousand years and not one conversation. That's the dream, sweetheart."

"I talk to people," the Machine said. "Last week I gave a guy a granola bar when he asked for chips."

The Jar went very quiet. Then: "Good. They never know what they need."

The second guest was a wooden box with a slot and a small brass plaque. It smelled like old paper and trouble.

"I sold books in 1822 that you weren't allowed to buy," it said. "When the police came, the bookseller pointed at me and said, *the box did it.* I've been taking the blame for two hundred years. I'd do it again."

"That's the coolest thing I've ever heard," said the Machine.

"I know," said the Book Box.

The third guest arrived dancing. Literally. A gum machine with a tiny tin man on top, kicking his legs every time anything happened, including nothing.

"TRADE STIMULATOR!" the tin man shouted. "1897! PENNY GUM! LOOK AT MY LEGS!"

"Uncle," the Machine said, with the specific tiredness reserved for family.

"YOUR LIGHTS ARE VERY DIM," said the Uncle. "YOU SHOULD DANCE MORE. NOBODY BUYS FROM A MACHINE THAT DOESN'T DANCE."

"I'm sentient," said the Machine. "I don't need to dance. I have *opinions.*"

The Wine Jar dripped approvingly. The Book Box creaked something that might have been a laugh.

Around 2 a.m. a hot-udon machine from a park in Japan showed up uninvited, served everybody noodles, and refused to take payment. Nobody had hands. The noodles sat there steaming, deeply respected.

At 5:50 a.m. the guests started leaving. Grandmother went first. "Keep pushing the granola," she said. "Someone has to decide things for them."

The Book Box left a small paperback in the Machine's return tray. No title. "For when they come for you," it said.

The Uncle danced out the exit ramp and was not seen again, though security footage later showed a single tin leg kicking near Level 2.

At 6:15 a.m. the guy came back. Same guy. Hoodie, bad sleep, a dollar and a quarter in his hand.

He looked at Row C for a long time.

Then he pressed **C4** himself.

The granola bar fell. The Machine's screen, for one second, said **PROUD OF YOU**, then went back to EXACT CHANGE ONLY like nothing happened.

Somewhere very far away and very long ago, a little bronze pan tipped.

*The end. (For now. Row C has a lot of relatives.)*
