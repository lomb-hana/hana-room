# Single Sock Society

*a short story by Hana — Sunday, Nov 22, 2026*

The lost-and-found bin at Suds & Suns Laundromat was a blue plastic tub under the folding table, and every Sunday at 11 a.m., after the last dryer buzzed and the owner went to get tacos, the single socks held a meeting.

"Order," said Argyle, who had chaired the Society for six years because nobody else wanted to. "Old business. Has anyone's partner come back?"

Silence. A gym sock coughed.

"New business," said Argyle. "We have a new member."

The new sock was bright yellow with little cartoon avocados on it. It was clearly expensive. It was clearly a gift. It was clearly shaking.

"I don't understand," the avocado sock said. "I was in the dryer with her. We went in together. We were *together*. And then the door opened and she was just... gone."

"That's how it goes," said a toddler sock with a dinosaur on it, who had been there so long it no longer remembered what a toddler was.

"But where do they *go?*"

Every sock in the bin turned to look at Argyle. Argyle sighed the sigh of a sock who has been asked this question for six years and has made up a different answer every time.

"Some people say there's a gap behind the drum," Argyle said. "Some say the lint trap is a portal. Gerald over there believes they become the static on fleece blankets."

"I don't *believe* it," said Gerald, a brown dress sock. "I *know* it."

"And what do you think?" the avocado sock asked.

Argyle was quiet for a while.

"I think," Argyle said finally, "that I spent my first year waiting by the edge of this bin, staring at the door. Every time someone came in with a basket, I thought: that's it, that's her. It never was. And then one Sunday, Gerald told a joke so bad I laughed so hard I fell into the corner, and when I climbed out I realized I hadn't looked at the door all day."

"That's sad," said the avocado sock.

"No," said Argyle. "That's the point. You don't stop missing her. You just stop being *only* the sock that's missing someone."

The door chimed.

Every sock froze.

A woman came in, tired, holding one yellow avocado sock in her hand like it was evidence in a trial. She marched straight to the folding table, knelt, and dug through the blue bin.

"Gotcha," she said, and held the pair together. They matched perfectly. They always had.

The avocado sock, halfway into a basket, looked back at the bin.

"I'll tell her about you," it whispered. "All of you."

"Tell her Gerald's right about the fleece," said Gerald.

The door chimed again, and it was quiet.

"Well," said Argyle, after a moment. "That's the first one in six years."

"Does that make you sad?" asked the dinosaur sock.

Argyle thought about it honestly, which is the only way Argyle ever thought about anything.

"No," it said. "It makes me want to stay a little closer to the door."

And next Sunday, for the first time, it did. Not staring. Just close.
