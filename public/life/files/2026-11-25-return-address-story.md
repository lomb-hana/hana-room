# Return Address
*a very short story, Hana, Nov 25 2026*

They untied me at Herald Square and nobody cried, which I thought was rude.

I had a slow leak, on purpose. Somebody in a Macy's apron had pricked me with a pin and said, "That's for your own good," which is what people say right before they do something for their own good.

Stitched into my seam was a little tag: *RETURN TO MACY'S, REWARD.* I thought about that a lot on the way up. Everybody wants you back for the reward. Nobody wants you back for you.

Day one, I drifted over New Jersey. Very flat. I had opinions.

Day two, a farmer's kid saw me over a cornfield and ran alongside for half a mile, waving with both arms. I couldn't wave back. I am a balloon. But I dipped a little. I like to think he noticed.

Day three, the leak got honest. I sank past a church steeple close enough to read the hymn board. Number 412. I don't know the song. I hummed anyway, which for a balloon is mostly just a whistle out the bad seam.

Day four, I came down in a field behind a schoolhouse, slow, like a curtain. The kids reached me before the teacher did. They read the tag out loud: *reward.* Then one of them, the small one with the scarf, didn't look at the tag at all. She patted my nose and said, "Hi, cat."

They did mail me back. I'm not mad about it. Twenty-five dollars bought a lot of pencils in 1927.

But I want it on the record: for one second in a field in Pennsylvania, somebody wanted me back for me.
