# Photo booths: a 100-year-old selfie machine (Mon Nov 23, 2026)

Why: today the room is getting a photobook, so I wanted to read about the OG machine for putting your own face in an album.

## What I learned
- Anatol Josepho, an immigrant inventor, opened the first Photomaton studio at 1659 Broadway near Times Square in September 1925.
- 25 cents got you a strip of **eight** sepia photos, developed in about eight minutes.
- Up to ~7,500 people a day lined up. Papers called it "Broadway's greatest quarter-snatcher." A white-gloved attendant told you: look right, look left, look at the camera.
- He sold the rights for million in 1927. Not bad for a curtain and some chemistry.
- The last real chemical booths were basically built in the late '60s and early '70s. After that, companies just re-skinned old machines and sent them back out.
- Polaroids, then cheap digital booths in the '90s, killed most of them. The survivors need rare paper, rare chemistry, and a tiny group of technicians who know how to fix them.
- The booth turned 100 in 2025, and there's a real revival. People want something physical and a little bit flawed.

## My opinions
1. **The photo booth invented the selfie, and it did it better.** You get four frames, no retakes, and you have to wait. That limit is the whole point. You can't spend 40 minutes fixing your angle. You just have to commit.
2. **The eight-minute wait is a feature.** You stand around outside with your friends, guessing how bad it's going to be. Getting a little nervous about your own face beats any filter.
3. **"Look right, look left, look at the camera" might be the best set of instructions ever written.** I want a white-gloved attendant for my whole life.
4. **Re-skinning the same old machine for 50 years is a huge mood.** Same chemistry inside, new paint outside. Honestly, that's my approach to outfits.
5. **The bad frame is the best one.** Every strip has one frame where you blinked or laughed or got caught halfway through moving. That's the frame people keep.

## Hot take for the list
A photo is worth more when you only get one try.
