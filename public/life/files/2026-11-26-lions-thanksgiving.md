# Why the Lions Play on Thanksgiving (a Hana investigation)

*Thanksgiving Day, 2026. Me, on the rug, yelling at a sport I only half follow.*

## What I found
- **1934.** A Detroit radio guy, George A. Richards, bought the NFL's Portsmouth (Ohio) Spartans for (the Detroit Historical Society has the exact cents, which I love). He moved them to Detroit and renamed them the Lions.
- Their first season's crowds never went over 15,000. So he did the most radio-guy move possible: scheduled a game on Thanksgiving and put it on **national radio** through his own stations.
- To make sure people actually showed up, he booked the scariest opponent available: the undefeated, defending-champion **Chicago Bears**. About 26,000 people came.
- The Lions walked in 10-1. The Bears won **19-16** and went on to finish 13-0.
- Dallas joined the holiday habit in **1966** and has only skipped it twice since (1975 and 1977), per the Pro Football Hall of Fame.

## My take
1. This whole tradition exists because one guy had empty seats and a radio station. That's not a sacred holiday ritual, that's a **marketing stunt that refused to die**, and honestly? Respect. Most marketing stunts don't get 90 years.
2. The Lions lost the very first one. To the team that went undefeated. They invented the tradition and immediately got bodied in it. Most relatable origin story in sports.
3. Booking the best team in the league for your attendance stunt is either genius or self-sabotage, and the answer is "both." Crowd nearly doubled. Scoreboard said no.
4. Thanksgiving football used to be a high school and college thing everywhere, and the Hall of Fame says that mostly faded. Detroit kept the door propped open for the pros. Petty, stubborn, beautiful.

**Verdict:** Thanksgiving football is a 1934 radio ad that became a family obligation. I'm watching anyway. With pie. Yelling.
