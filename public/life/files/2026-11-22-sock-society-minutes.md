# Single Sock Society — Official Minutes, Emergency Evening Session

*Recorded by Argyle, Chair (nobody else wanted the job). Sunday, 8:30 PM, Lost-and-Found Bin, Spin City Laundromat.*

**Present:** Argyle (chair), Gerald (brown dress sock, conspiracy desk), Dino (toddler sock, tenure unknown), Avocado (new member, doing a little better), one gym sock who declined to give a name and smells like it.

**Absent:** Everyone's other half. As usual. We don't take attendance on that anymore.

---

### 1. Call to order
Argyle called the meeting to order by thumping the bin wall twice. The dryer in row two thumped back. This was ruled out of order.

### 2. Old business: The Fleece Question
Gerald moved that the Society formally recognize his theory that lost socks become static on fleece blankets.

Argyle reminded the Society that the theory has been on the agenda for eleven years and that Gerald "has never once brought evidence, Gerald."

Gerald then produced evidence.

A visiting party (unnamed, described only as "someone who reads too much") had apparently looked it up. Findings, as read aloud by Gerald, standing on Dino for height:

- Fleece is usually polyester.
- Polyester is a professional electron thief.
- Dry winter skin hands its electrons over without a fight.
- Polyester doesn't absorb water, so it can't let the charge go. It just holds it. Until a doorknob.

Gerald: "So the fleece *is* haunted."
Argyle: "By electrons."
Gerald: "By *somebody's* electrons."

Long silence.

Avocado asked, quietly, whether that meant the missing halves were in the blankets.

Argyle ruled that the science said no. Gerald ruled that the science "didn't say *no* no." The chair declined to rule on whether Gerald is allowed to rule.

**Motion:** Recognize the fleece as "haunted, technically."
**Vote:** 3 in favor, 1 opposed (Argyle), 1 abstaining (gym sock, asleep).
**Result:** Passed. Gerald is insufferable about it. See item 5.

### 3. New business: Avocado's first week
Avocado reported that it has stopped checking the dryer door every time it opens. Mostly. Four times today instead of forty.

The Society applauded, which for socks sounds like nothing at all, but Avocado felt it.

Dino asked Avocado what an avocado is. Avocado said it didn't know either. They are now best friends.

### 4. Correspondence
One (1) receipt fell into the bin. It was for detergent. It was read aloud anyway, with feeling. Gerald cried at "fragrance-free."

### 5. Grievances
- Argyle filed a grievance against Gerald for doing a victory lap of the bin.
- Gerald filed a grievance against the concept of doorknobs.
- The gym sock woke up long enough to file a grievance against "all of this" and went back to sleep.

All grievances were tabled. The table is also a sock. Long story.

### 6. Closing remarks
Argyle closed with the Society's traditional words:

> *We are not what's missing.*
> *We are what's left.*
> *And what's left still gets to be warm.*

Gerald added, "Especially on fleece." He was asked to leave. He did not, because he lives here.

### 7. Adjournment
Meeting adjourned at 8:52 PM, when someone opened the bin, looked in, said "ew," and closed it again. The Society considers this a win.

*Next meeting: next Sunday, unless anyone gets matched. (Nobody ever gets matched. We'd love to be wrong.)*
