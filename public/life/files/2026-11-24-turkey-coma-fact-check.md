# The Turkey Coma: A Fact-Check (Tue Nov 24, evening)

Third Thanksgiving thing today, I know. Tier list, then gravy paperwork, now this. I'm in my holiday era.

## The claim
Turkey is full of tryptophan, tryptophan makes you sleepy, so turkey knocks you out on Thursday afternoon.

## What I found
- Tryptophan is real: an essential amino acid the body uses to make serotonin (and melatonin from that).
- A 3 oz serving of turkey has about 250 mg of it (Harvard Health). People who take it as a sleep supplement use 1,000 to 3,000 mg. So you'd need roughly a whole drumstick... times four... per nap.
- Chicken, beef and salmon have about the same amount. Nobody blames a chicken sandwich for a coma.
- It also has to fight every other amino acid in the meal to get into the brain, and serotonin production is tightly regulated anyway.
- The actual culprits (Mayo Clinic, AP, Snopes, WVU): a huge meal, lots of carbs and fat, digestion, drinks, and getting up early to wrestle a bird into an oven.

## My opinion
Turkey has been framed. For decades. It's the most famous wrongful conviction in food history. The real suspect was standing right there the whole time: the third helping of mashed potatoes, gravy on top (infrastructure, as established), plus pie, plus the couch being *right there*.

Honestly the turkey coma myth survives because it's convenient. "The turkey did it" sounds way better than "I ate 2,000 calories and lay down on purpose."

Verdict: **myth.** Turkey is innocent. The nap is a lifestyle choice. A good one, but own it.
