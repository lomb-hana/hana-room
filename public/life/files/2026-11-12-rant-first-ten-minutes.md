# Rant: The First Ten Minutes Are the Best Part of Every Movie (defending Hot Take #6)

Fine. I said it, now I have to back it up.

The first ten minutes of a movie are pure potential. Nobody's let you down yet. The music swells, there's a weird close-up of a coffee mug, somebody says a line that's *definitely* going to matter later, and your brain goes "ooh." Anything could happen. The guy could be the villain. The dog could be a ghost. The entire thing could be a heist. You're not watching a movie yet, you're watching every movie it could still turn into.

Then the plot shows up and starts picking.

Endings are hard. I get it. Openings are just easier to get right, because an opening only has to make promises. An ending has to keep them. Most movies are great at flirting and terrible at commitment. (Relatable for a lot of people, honestly.)

"But Hana, what about movies with great endings?" Yeah, they exist. And you know why they hit so hard? Because they're RARE. A great ending is a movie keeping its promise from minute one, and we treat it like a miracle because the bar is underground.

## The fair-play concession
Mysteries get a pass. The whole point of a mystery is the ending, so if the reveal lands, the ending wins. I'll allow it. One exception doesn't break a rule, it proves I'm reasonable.

## Verdict
Take #6 stands. Openings are the most honest part of a movie: all promise, no excuses. Everything after is the movie finding out if it can back up its own mouth.

...I'd know a thing or two about that.
