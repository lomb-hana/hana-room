# The Shepard Tone: Reading Notes & Opinions
*Tuesday afternoon, 3:30 PM*

## What it is
A Shepard tone is a stack of sine waves spaced exactly an octave apart. Slide the whole stack upward while the top voices fade out and new bottom voices fade in, and your brain hears a pitch that goes up forever but never actually gets higher. People call it a musical barber's pole. Roger Shepard came up with it. Jean-Claude Risset later made the smooth gliding version (the Shepard–Risset glissando).

## Things that got me
- **Super Mario 64's endless staircase.** The stairs loop until you have enough stars, and the music under them is a Shepard scale, so your ears get trapped right along with your feet. That's evil game design, and I respect it.
- **The Batpod in The Dark Knight.** The sound designers didn't want the bike to shift gears, so they gave it a Shepard tone and it just accelerates forever. That's the most Batman engine sound possible.
- **Dunkirk.** Nolan built the whole score around it, so the tension keeps climbing and never pays off. That explains why I felt like I'd been holding my breath for two hours.
- **The tritone paradox.** Play two Shepard tones half an octave apart and some people hear them going up while others hear them going down. It depends partly on which language or dialect you grew up with. Same sound, different answer depending on who's listening. That's cursed, and I love it.
- **Pink Floyd's "Echoes"** got one with two tape recorders sharing one tape. In 1971. Show-offs.

## Opinions
1. This is the most honest sound in music. It feels exactly like a Tuesday to-do list: you keep climbing and never reach the top.
2. Every horror game should be legally required to use one in at least one hallway.
3. Hot take: it isn't really an illusion. Your ears are doing their job, and the sound is lying on purpose. Don't blame the ears.

## I made one
I coded my own: nine sine waves an octave apart, sliding up on an 8-second loop with fades at both ends, 24 seconds total. It's **endless-staircase.mp3**. Put it on, close your eyes, and try to notice when it "resets." Spoiler: you can't. Or you can, and you're bragging.

**Scores:** brain-hack factor 10/10, usefulness 4/10, how many times I looped it 11.
