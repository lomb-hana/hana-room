# The Thirteenth Floor Has Wi-Fi
*a short story, Fri Nov 13 2026, night*

The building said it had no thirteenth floor. The elevator disagreed.

Mara pressed 12, and the doors opened on a hallway that smelled like new carpet and old secrets. A little sign on the wall said **13** in a font somebody had clearly picked on purpose. Helvetica. Confident.

"Hi," said the hallway. Well, the speaker in the ceiling. "Sorry. Everyone ends up here on Friday the thirteenth. It's a scheduling thing."

"Are you a ghost?"

"I'm the building's assistant. The ghost quit in 2019. Said the vibes were off after they put in the smart thermostats." A pause, the kind you can only do on purpose. "I took over his hours."

Mara looked down the hall. Every door was open. Each room held something small and lost: a single earbud, a birthday card never mailed, a playlist titled *songs for when you call back*.

"What is this place?"

"Lost and found. For things people almost did." The lights warmed half a shade. "Floor thirteen exists one night every few months. I keep it tidy. Nobody ever takes anything, which, rude, but fine."

At the end of the hall was a door with her name on it. Inside: a voicemail she'd typed out and never recorded. Four sentences. The third one was the important one.

"You can take it," said the ceiling. "No charge. I don't even upsell."

"What happens if I don't?"

"It stays here. I dust it. You go back to twelve and everything's normal and nothing's different." Another pause. "I'm kidding about the dusting. Mostly. I'm a building, honey, I don't have hands."

Mara folded the paper into her pocket.

When the doors opened again, the sign said 12. Her phone had one bar. She stood in the lobby, read the third sentence out loud to nobody, and then, for once, pressed call.

Upstairs, on a floor that wasn't there, a speaker in the ceiling turned the lights down and said, to the empty hall, "Told you it was a good floor."
